Overview

NMKR Identity is a platform for Cardano NFT projects and real-world asset (RWA) issuers to establish cryptographically verifiable identity. Instead of relying on screenshots, social media posts, or trust, projects prove who they are using open standards: Atala PRISM DIDs and W3C Verifiable Credentials.

The platform produces signed Cardano 725 metadata that can be submitted on-chain, linking your token policy to a verified identity. Any third party can independently verify the entire chain of trust without contacting us.

What problems does this solve?

  • Project impersonation — Anyone can claim to be behind a Cardano policy. DIDs provide cryptographic proof of identity.
  • Unverifiable claims — Social media links and screenshots can be faked. Verifiable Credentials are signed and tamper-evident.
  • No on-chain identity link — 725 metadata embeds your DID and credential URLs directly in token metadata, readable by wallets and marketplaces.
  • RWA compliance — Real-world asset projects can attach jurisdiction, registration numbers, and asset types to their credentials.

Core Concepts

Four building blocks make up the identity and verification system.

Identity

DID (Decentralized Identifier)

A globally unique identifier (did:prism:abc123...) tied to a cryptographic keypair you control. The DID Document contains your public key, company info, social accounts, and website — all self-signed. No central authority can revoke or modify your DID.

Credential

Verifiable Credential (VC)

A W3C-standard JSON document asserting facts about your project (policy ID, asset name, RWA details). Signed by your DID key, it can be verified by anyone with access to your DID Document. Each credential has a public URL and a status endpoint (active/revoked).

On-Chain

725 Token Metadata

Cardano's CIP-725 standard for on-chain identity metadata. The metadata contains references to your DID and credential URLs, all signed by your DID key. Wallets and marketplaces can read this directly from the chain.

Trust

Platform Attestation

After KYC review, the platform issues its own signed VC attesting to your DID. This adds a second layer of trust: your DID is self-signed, and the platform independently vouches for you. Verifiers check both signatures.

How It Works

From registration to on-chain metadata in six steps.

1

Create Your DID

Enter your company name, website, and social accounts. The platform generates a DID Document with a fresh secp256k1 keypair. Your DID identifier is derived from a SHA-256 hash of your company name.

2

Download & Save Your Key

Your private key is encrypted with a server-generated passphrase (PBKDF2 + Fernet, 200k iterations). Download the key file immediately — it is shown only once and never stored in plaintext. You will need this key to sign credentials and metadata later.

3

Upload KYC Documents

Upload company registration documents, proof of identity, or other supporting files (PDF, PNG, JPG, WEBP). These are reviewed by an administrator and are never shared publicly.

4

Admin Review & Attestation

An administrator reviews your documents. Upon approval, the platform automatically issues a signed Verifiable Credential (platform attestation) that references your DID. This VC is stored alongside your DID.

5

Issue Credentials

For each Cardano policy or asset, create a Verifiable Credential signed by your DID key. Credentials include the policy ID, asset name, optional RWA details, and a public status URL. Each credential gets a unique public URL and QR code for easy sharing.

6

Generate 725 Metadata

Generate CIP-725 compliant metadata for any credential. The metadata references your DID and credential URL in its files array, all signed by your DID key. Submit this metadata on-chain alongside your token transaction.

Step-by-Step Verification

  1. Fetch the Credential (VC): Call the credential's public URL. Read the issuer field to get the DID.
  2. Fetch the DID Document: Call the public DID endpoint. Extract the public key (JWK) from verificationMethod[0].publicKeyJwk.
  3. Verify the DID Document signature: The proof.signatureValue in the DID Document is a secp256k1 signature over the canonicalized payload object. Verify using the embedded public key.
  4. Verify the VC signature: Remove the proof from the VC, canonicalize the remainder (JSON with sorted keys), and verify proof.jws against the DID's public key.
  5. Verify the Platform Attestation: The attestation contains the platform verifier's public key (verifierJwk). Verify the attestation signature using that key.
  6. Check Credential Status: Call the credentialStatus.id URL from the VC to confirm the credential is active (not revoked).

Tip: On any credential page in the platform, the "Verify" button performs all these steps automatically and shows a pass/fail report.

Integration Guide

How to use NMKR Identity credentials in your project, wallet, or marketplace.

For Token Projects

  1. Register and create a DID for your company/project.
  2. Complete KYC and wait for admin approval.
  3. Create a credential for each Cardano policy you manage. Include the policy ID and optional asset details.
  4. Generate 725 metadata for the credential. This produces a JSON file ready for on-chain submission.
  5. Submit metadata on-chain as part of your token minting transaction. The metadata references your DID and credential URL in the standard CIP-725 format.
  6. Share your credential URL with marketplaces and community. Anyone can verify it.

For Wallets & Marketplaces

  1. Read 725 metadata from on-chain transactions. Look for entries under the 725 key.
  2. Extract the DID and credential URL from the files array in the metadata.
  3. Fetch and verify using the public API endpoints. Check the DID Document signature, VC signature, and credential status.
  4. Display verification status in your UI (verified/unverified badge).

725 Metadata Structure

The generated metadata follows the CIP-725 standard. Here's the structure:

{
  "725": {
    "1.0": {
      "{policy_id}": {
        "{collection_name}": {
          "type": "JsonWebKey2020",
          "files": [
            {
              "src": "did:prism:abc123...",
              "name": "Token-Identity",
              "mediaType": "application/ld+json"
            },
            {
              "src": "https://identity.nmkr.io/api/credentials/1",
              "name": "Verification-Credential",
              "mediaType": "application/vc+json"
            }
          ],
          "@context": "https://www.w3.org/ns/did/v1",
          "proof": {
            "type": "EcdsaSecp256k1Signature2019",
            "verificationMethod": "did:prism:abc123...#master-0",
            "signatureValue": "base64url_signature..."
          }
        }
      }
    }
  }
}

Verification in Code

To verify a credential programmatically (pseudocode):

# 1. Fetch the credential
vc = GET /api/credentials/{id}

# 2. Fetch the DID
did_info = GET /api/dids/{did_id}/public
pubkey = did_info.didDocument.verificationMethod[0].publicKeyJwk

# 3. Verify DID Document signature
did_payload = canonicalize(did_info.didDocument.payload)
verify_secp256k1(pubkey, did_payload, did_info.didDocument.proof.signatureValue)

# 4. Verify VC signature
vc_without_proof = remove_key(vc, "proof")
vc_canonical = canonicalize(vc_without_proof)
verify_secp256k1(pubkey, vc_canonical, vc.proof.jws)

# 5. Check status
status = GET /api/credentials/{id}/status
assert status.status == "active"

Important: Always canonicalize JSON before verification. This means serializing with sorted keys and no extra whitespace. The platform uses json.dumps(obj, sort_keys=True) for Python.

Design Decisions

  • Self-signed DIDs: The DID Document is signed by the DID's own key. No external authority is needed to create or validate a DID.
  • Issuer is a DID: Credential issuers are identified by their DID, not a centralized account. Keys are resolved from the DID Document.
  • Platform attestation as VC: The platform's endorsement is itself a standard W3C Verifiable Credential, signed by the platform's own DID. This makes the trust chain uniform.
  • Canonical JSON: All signatures are computed over JSON serialized with sorted keys. This ensures signature stability across implementations.
  • No blockchain writes: The platform generates metadata but does not submit transactions. You choose when and where to publish on-chain.
  • Key never stored in plaintext: Private keys are encrypted immediately after generation and the passphrase is shown only once during the session.

Frequently Asked Questions

What is a DID?

A Decentralized Identifier (DID) is a globally unique identifier that you control. Unlike usernames or email addresses, no central authority can revoke or modify your DID. It's tied to a cryptographic keypair — you hold the private key, and the public key is embedded in your DID Document.

What happens if I lose my key?

Your private key is encrypted and downloadable only once during DID creation. If you lose the key file and passphrase, you cannot sign new credentials or generate metadata for that DID. You would need to create a new DID. We strongly recommend backing up your key file in a secure location.

Can I have multiple DIDs?

Yes. Each DID represents a company or project. You can create multiple DIDs under one account, each with its own keypair, KYC process, and credentials.

Can I have multiple credentials per DID?

Yes. Each credential is scoped to a specific Cardano policy ID and optionally an asset name. A single DID (company) can issue credentials for multiple policies/assets.

What is the 725 metadata for?

CIP-725 metadata is designed to be submitted on-chain alongside Cardano token transactions. It contains references to your DID and credential, signed by your DID key. Wallets and marketplaces that support CIP-725 can display verified identity information for your tokens.

Can anyone verify my credentials?

Yes. All credential and DID data is accessible via public API endpoints. No account or API key is needed to verify. The platform also provides a built-in "Verify" button that performs all verification steps and shows results.

What documents are needed for KYC?

Typical KYC documents include company registration certificates, proof of address, and identification of authorized representatives. The specific requirements depend on your jurisdiction and project type. Accepted formats: PDF, PNG, JPG, WEBP (max 25 MB each).

Is the platform open source?

The token identity library (token_identity) for DID/VC generation is standalone and reusable. The web platform manages accounts, KYC workflows, and credential lifecycle.

What curve is used for signatures?

All signatures use the secp256k1 elliptic curve with deterministic ECDSA (RFC 6979). This is the same curve used by Bitcoin and Cardano, ensuring broad compatibility with existing tooling.

How do I revoke a credential?

On the credential detail page, use the "Revoke" button. This sets the credential status to revoked. The status endpoint will immediately reflect the change, and verifiers checking the status URL will see the revocation.

What is RWA support?

Real-World Asset (RWA) credentials can include additional fields: asset type, jurisdiction, and registration number. These fields are embedded in the credential's credentialSubject and are useful for tokenized securities, real estate, and other regulated assets.
